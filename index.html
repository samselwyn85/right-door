Costs and rules

What a pharmacy visit costs

  • Advice from a pharmacist is free.
  • Funded consultations (for the conditions marked in this tool): up to $20, or up to $5 with a Community Services Card.
  • Funded medicine: $5 each for ages 14 to 64. Free for under 14s, people 65 and over, and Community Services Card holders.
  • Emergency contraception: the consultation is free if you are under 30.
  • Not every pharmacy offers the funded service, and some medicines need a specially trained pharmacist. Phone ahead.
  • If you are outside the funded group you can often still buy the treatment at the normal price. This does not apply to UTI antibiotics or the contraceptive pill: for those you need a prescriber.

These usually need a prescriber

  • Antibiotics for chest, ear, sinus, skin or tooth infections, and for most sore throats
  • UTIs in men, children, pregnancy, or women over 65
  • Asthma inhalers (a pharmacist can only give an emergency supply of one you have been prescribed before), and new or changed long-term medicines
  • Strong steroid creams such as betamethasone or mometasone
  • Codeine and other strong pain relief
  • Shingles antivirals, and treatment for sexually transmitted infections
  • Starting the contraceptive pill for the first time

Repeats are easier now

Since 1 February 2026 a prescriber can write a prescription for up to 12 months if your long-term condition is stable. You collect up to 3 months at a time from the same pharmacy. Ask at your next appointment.

What a prescription costs

$5 per medicine. Free for under 14s, people 65 and over, Community Services Card holders, and once you, your partner and your dependent children under 18 have paid for 20 items in a year (1 February to 31 January). GP visits are free for enrolled children under 14 at nearly every practice.

About Right Door

Important. This is a general guide to help you choose where to go. It is not medical advice and it cannot diagnose you. A pharmacist, nurse or doctor will make the final call, and a pharmacist may still refer you to a GP.

In an emergency call 111. If you are worried, symptoms are getting worse, or you are not sure, call Healthline free on 0800 611 116 at any time.
